Project background

ApexPost Logistics is a national postal, shipping and retail services provider with thousands of physical counter locations. Until now, its branches have used single-purpose legacy terminals that only weigh mail and select stamps.

ApexPost is moving to a unified retail business model. Each branch will now act as a micro-fulfilment centre. From one counter, it sells retail goods (packaging materials, stationery and office supplies) alongside parcel and letter shipping, money orders and identity verification services.

From this week onwards, your continuous project is the ApexPost Counter Platform. It replaces the TechNova Resource Management System from Week 1. Every later week (database, APIs, cloud, mobile app with .NET MAUI, DevSecOps) builds on what you deliver now, so design deliberately.

Week 2 goal

Refactor your Week 1 solution to the ApexPost domain and redo the Week 1 scope for the new business. Keep your architecture if it still fits. Redesign it if it does not, and explain why in an ADR.
Add an ASP.NET Core MVC web application on top of your domain and services.
Use advanced C# 14, custom and built-in middleware, and action filters as much as you can. Use them to make the platform:
Secure: protect users, data and integration endpoints.
Fast: responsive under load, with no blocking calls and with bounded concurrency.
Auditable: every important business action is traceable to a user, a branch, a time and a correlation ID.
Part A: Redo the Week 1 scope for ApexPost (functional requirements)

Your system must support at least the following.

Branches and counters

Each branch has a branch code, a name, a region, opening hours, an active/closed status and one or more counter terminals.
Staff

Add, update and deactivate staff.
Each staff member has an ID, a name, an email, a role, a home branch and an active status.
Roles: Counter Clerk, Branch Manager, Auditor and Administrator.
Retail catalogue and inventory

Products have a SKU, a name, a category (packaging, stationery, office supplies), a unit price and a tax category.
Stock is held per branch.
Postal and financial services

Shipping: parcel or letter, weight, dimensions, destination zone and service level (Standard, Express, Registered). The price comes from a pricing strategy that you design.
Money orders: amount, sender, recipient and fee. Amounts above a configurable threshold require identity verification.
Identity verification: record the document type and a verification result. Never store the full document number. Store it masked (for example, last 4 characters only).
Counter transactions (basket)

A single transaction can mix retail items and services.
It has a branch, a counter, a clerk, line items, totals, tax, a payment method and a status (Open, Paid, Voided, Refunded).
Business rules (must be enforced)

Branch stock can never go negative. A sale fails if stock is insufficient.
Inactive staff cannot open or process transactions. A clerk can only transact at their assigned branch.
A money order above the threshold cannot be completed without a successful identity verification in the same transaction.
Parcels over the maximum weight for their service level are rejected.
Voids and refunds require the Branch Manager role and a reason.
A closed branch cannot accept transactions.
Reports (at least four)

Daily branch sales split by retail and services.
Low-stock report per branch, with a threshold you define.
Shipping volume by zone and service level.
Money orders and identity verifications per branch (the auditor view).
Persistence and notifications

Persistence: still no database this week. Use JSON files behind repository abstractions, so that next week’s database can replace them without changing business logic.
Notifications (simulated): for low stock, for a high-value money order and for every void or refund. Use events or an observer.
Part B: Advanced C# 14 (show where each one solves a real problem)

Use and justify at least 8 of the following:

Records and value objects: Money, Weight, Address, BranchCode, Sku.
Pattern matching: for pricing, tax rules and service-level checks.
C# 14 extension members: for example, decimal rounding helpers for Money, or summary properties on a basket. The field keyword wherever it removes boilerplate.
Generics with meaningful constraints: a generic repository, plus a specification or filter for reports.
Delegates and Func/Predicate: for configurable business rules, such as discount, pricing or low-stock rules.
Events: TransactionCompleted, StockLow, MoneyOrderIssued, TransactionVoided.
Custom attributes with a reflection-based validator: cache the reflection metadata.
async/await with CancellationToken end to end: from the controller down to the JSON repository.
Task.WhenAll: for independent work, for example building the branch dashboard (sales, stock and alerts in parallel).
SemaphoreSlim: to limit concurrent access to the simulated courier-rate service and to safe JSON file writes.
ValueTask and an in-memory cache: for frequently read data, such as zone rates or the product catalogue. Measure or explain why it helps.
Cancellation versus failure: treat cancellation separately from failures, and use timeouts for slow external lookups (simulated courier tracking).
Part C: ASP.NET Core MVC front end

A login page. A branch dashboard. A product catalogue and stock view. A new counter transaction screen (add items and services, take payment). A shipping label preview. Money order and identity verification forms. Report pages.
Use conventional routing. Use the Post/Redirect/Get pattern after every successful POST. Return correct action results: View, RedirectToAction, NotFound, BadRequest, Forbid, JSON and status codes.
Keep controllers thin. Business logic stays in your domain and service layers.
Part D: Middleware pipeline (security, performance and observability)

Build and register a deliberate pipeline. Explain the order in DESIGN.md. At a minimum, include the following.

Global exception handling that returns Problem Details: a safe message plus a trace ID, with no stack traces in production.
Correlation ID middleware: reuse an incoming X-Correlation-Id header or generate one. Echo it in the response and add it to every log entry.
Request logging and request timing middleware: use structured logging. Use try/finally so failed requests are timed as well.
Maintenance mode middleware: controlled by configuration. It returns 503 with Retry-After and keeps /health available.
Security headers middleware: CSP, X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options and Referrer-Policy (set using OnStarting). HTTPS redirection, plus HSTS outside Development.
API key validation for integration endpoints, such as /integration/parcel-status (used by courier partners):
Mark protected endpoints with endpoint metadata (a custom attribute).
Read the key from configuration or user-secrets, never from source code.
Compare keys in constant time.
Return 401 for a missing or invalid key, and 500 if the key is not configured.
IP restriction middleware for admin and integration endpoints. Use an allowlist from configuration and return 403. Document the forwarded-headers and proxy risk.
Cookie authentication with claims and roles:
Claims: StaffId, Role, BranchCode.
Authorization policies, for example “BranchManagerOnly” and “SameBranchOnly”.
Antiforgery on every form POST.
Cookie settings: HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite.
Rate limiting: a strict policy on the login POST (brute-force protection) and a per-partner policy on integration endpoints. Both return 429.
CORS: only the approved partner tracking-widget origin may call the parcel-status endpoint. Explain why CORS is not a security boundary.
Health check endpoint: /health.
Part E: Action filters (auditing and cross-cutting MVC concerns)

Implement at least three filters and explain why each one is a filter rather than middleware.

AuditActionFilter (IAsyncActionFilter, registered with ServiceFilter or globally):
It records who did what: StaffId, role, branch, controller and action, key route values, the result status, duration and correlation ID.
It covers sales, voids, refunds, money orders, identity verification and stock adjustments.
Write the audit log to an append-only JSON audit store behind an IAuditStore interface.
Never write passwords, full ID document numbers, API keys or card data to the audit log.
Validation filter: returns a consistent BadRequest or redisplays the view when the model state is invalid.
Performance filter: logs a warning for actions slower than a configurable threshold.
Optional: a BranchAccessFilter that blocks access to another branch’s data, or an exception filter for domain exceptions.

xUnit unit tests (required) for: the stock rule, the money order identity-verification rule, parcel weight limits and pricing.

Security headers are present.

The X-Correlation-Id header is returned.
